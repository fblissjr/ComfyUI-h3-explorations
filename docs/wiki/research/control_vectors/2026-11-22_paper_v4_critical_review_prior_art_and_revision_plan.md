## 1. Dissecting the "Magic Math Equation" (`Theorem 1`) & Two Core Algorithms (`Algorithms 1–2`)

### 1.1 What `Theorem 1` Actually Means — and What It Does Not Mean

On `2026-11-20`, the initial unpooled slider prototype (`M3_subspace`) regressed centered Pole-A `diff_span` token states $\mathbf{A}_c \in \mathbb{R}^{N_A \times D}$ onto centered matched shifts $\mathbf{D}_c = \mathbf{B}_c - \mathbf{A}_c$ via rank-$r$ truncated-input ridge regression:

$$\mathbf{W}_D = \mathbf{V}_r^\top \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_i^2 + \lambda}\right) \mathbf{U}_r^\top (\mathbf{B}_c - \mathbf{A}_c) \;\equiv\; \underbrace{\mathbf{V}_r^\top \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_i}{\sigma_i^2 + \lambda}\right) \mathbf{U}_r^\top \mathbf{B}_c}_{\mathbf{W}_B\;\text{(Pole-B cross-covariance)}} \;-\; \underbrace{\mathbf{V}_r^\top \operatorname{diag}\!\left(\frac{\sigma_i^2}{\sigma_i^2 + \lambda}\right) \mathbf{V}_r}_{\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{V}_r,\lambda}\;\text{(Tikhonov Pole-A projector)}}$$

**Plain-English reality check:**
1. **The equation is NOT a new mathematical theorem.** Distributing a linear regression operator across a difference $\mathbf{B}_c - \mathbf{A}_c$ and applying $\mathbf{U}_r^\top \mathbf{A}_c = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r \mathbf{V}_r^\top$ is textbook Tikhonov linear algebra. The `5.862e-14` verification in [`bench/verify_pole_a_erasure.py:96-109`](../../../../bench/verify_pole_a_erasure.py#L96-L109) checks floating-point equality of two algebraically identical rewritings of the same formula.
2. **ColBERT plays ZERO mathematical role in $-\mathbf{P}_{\mathbf{V}_r,\lambda}$.** The identity holds for *any* matrix $\mathbf{B}_c$ regardless of how tokens were matched. Indeed, the shipped builder [`control_vectors.py:1333-1358`](../../../../control_vectors.py#L1333-L1358) computes `erasure.vr` and `erasure.diag` purely from the thin SVD of centered `a_spans` without ever referencing `b_spans` or soft-MaxSim! ColBERT row-softmax (`tau = 0.15`) enters **only** into the 1D mean boost vector `erasure.mu_d` (`control_vectors.py:1311-1331`).
3. **Why it felt like a breakthrough — and what its real value actually was:** Before writing out the split, the `2026-11-20` session assumed `M3` worked because $\mathbf{W}_D$ learned a rich per-token **Pole-B mapping** from input scene tokens to output target tokens. Evaluating both sides on H3's `7` cached `.pt` banks proved that hypothesis **wrong**: because diverse Pole-A baselines are largely uncorrelated with a fixed Pole-B style, $\|\text{ctx}_{-\mathbf{A}}\|_2 / \|\text{ctx}_{\mathbf{B}}\|_2 \in [4.2, 10.3]$ and $\cos(\text{ctx}_D, -\mathbf{A}) \in [0.9686, 0.9960]$ — meaning **`97%–99.6%` of `M3`'s runtime shift was simply deleting Pole A's conflicting subspace**! That **empirical diagnostic / self-correction** — followed by dropping $\mathbf{W}_B$, switching to prompt-local centering $\bar{\mathbf{h}}$, adding $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_d$, clamping polarity to $[0, 1]$, orthogonalizing holds, and RMS-matching to $|s|\tau_{\text{rms}}$ (**Algorithms 1–2**) — was genuinely valuable science, **not** a new linear-algebra theorem.

### 1.2 Three Formal Mathematical Bugs in Paper v4's Theorems (`lines 188–212`)

A strict theoretical reviewer reading §5 of Paper v4 will immediately catch three formal errors on disk:

1. **Bug 1 — Theorem 1 misstates Principal Components Ridge Regression (`PCRR`) as Reduced-Rank Ridge Regression (`lines 188–198`):**
   - Line 190 defines $\mathbf{W}_D = \arg\min_{\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{W}) \le r}\|\mathbf{D}_c - \mathbf{A}_c \mathbf{W}\|_F^2 + \lambda\|\mathbf{W}\|_F^2$.
   - The true minimizer of the rank-constrained ridge problem is **Reduced-Rank Ridge Regression** (Mukherjee & Zhu, 2011): compute the unconstrained ridge predictions $\hat{\mathbf{D}}_{\text{ridge}} = \mathbf{A}_c(\mathbf{A}_c^\top\mathbf{A}_c + \lambda\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{A}_c^\top \mathbf{D}_c$, then project onto the top-$r$ right singular vectors of $\hat{\mathbf{D}}_{\text{ridge}}$ — **not** the top-$r$ right singular vectors $\mathbf{V}_r$ of $\mathbf{A}_c$!
   - Paper v4's proof (`line 194`) quietly turns the rank-$r$ *approximation* $\mathbf{A}_c \approx \mathbf{U}_r \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r \mathbf{V}_r^\top$ (`line 188`, where $r = 8 \ll \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}_c) \approx 169$ on `noir`) into an *exact equality* $\mathbf{A}_c = \mathbf{U}_r \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r \mathbf{V}_r^\top$.
   - What [`bench/verify_pole_a_erasure.py:96-98`](../../../../bench/verify_pole_a_erasure.py#L96-L98) actually verifies (`5.862e-14`) is the **PCRR estimator**:
     $$\mathbf{W}_{\text{PCRR}} = \arg\min_{\operatorname{row}(\mathbf{W}) \subseteq \operatorname{row}(\mathbf{V}_r)} \|\mathbf{D}_c - \mathbf{A}_c \mathbf{W}\|_F^2 + \lambda\|\mathbf{W}\|_F^2 \;=\; \arg\min_{\mathbf{W}} \|\mathbf{D}_c - \mathbf{A}_{c,r} \mathbf{W}\|_F^2 + \lambda\|\mathbf{W}\|_F^2.$$
   - **Exact v5 fix:** Change the constraint in Theorem 1 from $\operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{W}) \le r$ to $\operatorname{row}(\mathbf{W}) \subseteq \operatorname{row}(\mathbf{V}_r)$ (and note that $\mathbf{U}_r^\top \mathbf{A}_c = \boldsymbol{\Sigma}_r \mathbf{V}_r^\top$ holds *exactly* by full-SVD orthogonality $\mathbf{U}_r^\top \mathbf{U}_\perp = \mathbf{0}$ even when $r < \operatorname{rank}(\mathbf{A}_c)$), and demote it to **Lemma 1 (PCRR Split Diagnostic)**.
2. **Bug 2 — Theorem 2.3's "Pre-Normalization Non-Inversion Bound" (`line 209`) is silently broken by Step 5's outer RMS renormalization (`line 253`):**
   - Theorem 2.3 guarantees that *before* Step 5, each Pole-A coordinate $z_{i,k}$ scales by $1 - \alpha\beta_{\text{cap}}g_k \in (0, 1]$.
   - However, Step 5 rescales the entire $\Delta = s\mathbf{1}\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_d^\top - \alpha\beta_{\text{cap}}\mathbf{E}_{\text{raw}}$ by $\gamma = \frac{|s|\tau_{\text{rms}}}{\operatorname{RMS}(\Delta)}$. Whenever $\mathbf{U}_{\text{hold}}$ projects out a substantial share of $\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_d$ or $\mathbf{E}_{\text{raw}}$ partially anti-aligns with $s\hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_d$ across active rows, $\operatorname{RMS}(\Delta) < |s|\tau_{\text{rms}} \implies \gamma > 1$, so the **actual delivered** coordinate multiplier $1 - \gamma\alpha\beta_{\text{cap}}g_k$ **can turn negative (sign-invert)**!
   - **Exact v5 fix:** State explicitly the sufficient condition for *post-normalization* non-inversion ($\gamma \le 1$, guaranteed when $\mathbf{U}_{\text{hold}} = \emptyset$ and $\mathbf{E}_{\text{raw}} \perp \hat{\boldsymbol{\mu}}_d$, or enforced strictly by clamping $\gamma \le 1$ on the erasure term separately from the boost term).
3. **Bug 3 — Theorem 2.5 (`line 211`) omits eigenvalue clamping and contradicts §7.1 (`line 425`):**
   - Theorem 2.5 writes $\mathbf{M} = \sum_{k=1}^K \alpha_k \mathbf{V}_{r,k}^\top \operatorname{diag}(\mathbf{g}_k)\mathbf{V}_{r,k}$ without clamping eigenvalues to $[0, 1]$ (which is why stacking `noir + chaos_panic + de_llm_slop` produced `6` eigenvalues `> 1.0`, peaking at `1.8568`, in `showcase_a_clamped_stacking.json` until commit `0c8b1414` added clamping!).
   - Moreover, truncating at $r_{\text{union}} = 16$ preserves the exact direct sum of **$K = 2$** rank-8 sliders (`2 × 8 = 16`), contradicting §7.1's claim of **`"5+ simultaneous sliders"`** (`5 × 8 = 40 > 16`).
