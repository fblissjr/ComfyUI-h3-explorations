# Sol-Attn's measured arms: speed, the sigma window, and quality

Detail page of [`sol_attn.md`](sol_attn.md). Each arm says what it ran on. Arms before 2026-08-14 ran the deleted
Triton backend; arms before 2026-09-27 ran nodes and knobs that no longer exist. None of them is a measurement of the
recipe that ships today unless it says so.

## The sigma window is not a step fraction

`start_percent` and `end_percent` pass through `percent_to_sigma`, which for a flow model is
`time_snr_shift(shift, 1 - percent)`. H3 runs `shift_video=12.0`, which bends it hard. For `simple`, with
`end_percent` 0.9 (the value before 2026-09-11):

| start | 16 steps | 20 steps |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 15/16 sparse (94%) | 19/20 (95%) |
| 0.1 | 13/16 (81%) | 17/20 (85%) |
| **0.2 (shipped)** | **11/16 (69%)** | **14/20 (70%)** |
| 0.3 | 10/16 (62%) | 13/20 (65%) |
| 0.4 | 8/16 (50%) | 11/20 (55%) |

`end_percent` 1.0 (shipped since 2026-09-11) adds exactly the one trailing step back to each column. 0.2 to 0.3 costs
one step of 16: not linear. Priced by arithmetic, 0.2 forces a flat quarter of evaluations dense at every step count
(4 of 16, 2 of 8, 1 of 4), because it is a fixed fraction of a schedule uniform in base sigma. This is also why the
scheduler is pinned to `simple`: another scheduler puts a different number of steps inside the same band, so a
scheduler A/B that ignores it measures two things.

## The unit trap

Upstream reported CUDA at 1.4x over Triton end to end, and the old `centroid_tail` tooltip said "~1.4x faster" about
the operation. Pairing the two is numerology across denominators: when only the kernel changes, end-to-end speedup
cannot exceed kernel speedup. **Never quote a `bench_minimax_attn.py` number against a `bench_e2e_h3.py` one.**
Attention is part of a step, and Sol runs only inside its window.

## Timed arms, oldest last

**Selection, 2026-08-22: top-k against adaptive tau.** Six arms, one graph, matched seeds, alternating, two timed
runs each on a fresh server, 1344x768, 362 frames, the subway scene, kernel `0.2.31+sol.23d1a66`. Top-k at keep 15%
was 4.7% faster than adaptive tau at 1.0, keep 10% 10.3% faster; the Turbo-SLA LoRA made no difference (within
0.06 s). Record:
[`data/results/2026-08-22_sol_selection_verdict_subway.json`](../../data/results/2026-08-22_sol_selection_verdict_subway.json).
An earlier run the same day showed the opposite sign and did not replicate after a restart; the rule it produced is
in [`../method/evidence.md`](../method/evidence.md): do not time an arm set on a long-lived session. The clips showed
a failure neither selection owns: in 11 of 12, a character introduced after a cut takes over the singing of the one
before it
([`data/results/2026-08-22_subway_speaker_bleed.json`](../../data/results/2026-08-22_subway_speaker_bleed.json)), a
finding about the model and prompt. Top-k is retired with the SLA lane; under it Sol still added a pooled term for
every unpicked block, so it was a third attention, never the hard top-k router the Turbo-SLA LoRA was distilled under.

**Sage mode against Sol reachability, 2026-08-18.** Six arms, one variable each. The data is
[`bench/results/2026-08-18_attention_defaults.json`](../../bench/results/2026-08-18_attention_defaults.json),
generated by `bench/make_attention_defaults_json.py`; read the ratios there. It moved the shipped sage mode to `auto`
(`h3_config.SAGE_NODE`'s comment has why) and showed Sol's own per-step cost does not move with the sage mode, so the
whole fp16 difference sat in the dense steps. The board power limit was not stock: ratios hold, absolute seconds do
not compare to earlier runs.

**First CUDA end-to-end, 2026-08-14.** 1344x768, 362 frames, 16 steps, `res_multistep`/`simple`, two runs plus a
discarded warmup, kernel `0.2.31+sol.c04ef20`:

| arm | sampler | vs sage |
|---|---|---|
| sage only | 794.7 s | 1.000x |
| Sol on | 493.4 s | 1.611x |
| Sol on, `centroid_tail=0` | 505.8 s | 1.571x |
| Sol on, `reuse_qkv_memory=1` | 493.5 s | 1.610x |

Run-to-run spread 0.1-0.12%. The sage arm ran `fp8_cuda++`, not the fp16 mode the graphs then shipped, and sage ran
5 of 16 steps in the Sol arm too, so the drift hit both arms by different amounts: the direction understates, the
magnitude is not pinned. Both toggles are gone from the kernel.

**Kernel level, T=512 synthetic, tau 1.3**: CUDA 0.999919 from the reference, Triton INT8 0.999885, Triton bf16
0.999995, each graded in its own tail mode (grading against the wrong tail mode costs about 1.2e-3, more than the gap
between backends). `reuse_qkv_memory` was numerically identical to the normal entry. Fidelity, not accuracy.

**Triton, 362 frames, 1344x768, 20 steps** (above the floor; same seed, warmup discarded, alternating):

| arm | sampler | vs sage |
|---|---|---|
| sage only | 991.0 s | 1.00x |
| sol, no int8, tau 1.2 | 827.9 s | 1.20x |
| sol + `int8_qk` + `int8_pv`, tau 1.2 | 714.9 s | 1.39x |
| + tau 1.6 | 648.2 s | 1.53x |
| + tau 2.0 | 602.9 s | 1.64x |

Sparsity and int8 are separate levers of comparable size. The tau 1.6 and 2.0 rows are above where the
object-dissolve artifact appears and are not recommendations. The Triton-era guidance was to stay at or below about 1.3, which cost 82.3 s against tau 2.0 at this length (712.1
against 629.8 s) and was judged worth it. Upstream's Triton tooltip said int8 helps at `tau<=1.5` and becomes a net loss at `tau>=2.0`.

**Triton, 124 frames, 20 steps: below the floor**, 37,296 tokens; kept for the record and not a baseline. No sage
342.1 s, sage 178.7 s (1.91x), sage + sol `int8_qk` 155.0 s (1.15x over sage). Attention was about 50% of the step
here against about 76% at 362. The Sol build was not recorded.

**Triton profile, one forward, 50 blocks, device time:** sage dense attention 4296 ms (85.9 ms a block), Sol sparse
`int8_qk` 2668 ms (53.4 ms); Sol routing and quant prep 161 ms (2.1%); non-attention time matched to 0.2% (4944
against 4935 ms), the control that the patching surface was clean.

## Quality

Nothing here is a blind judgement of the shipped recipe. The judged panels that decided today's defaults are on the
pages that own those knobs: the 2026-10-02 sink and `dense_blocks` panel ([`sol_dense_blocks.md`](sol_dense_blocks.md)),
the 2026-10-01 and 2026-10-05 `start_percent` records ([`sol_attn.md`](sol_attn.md), the knob table), and the
2026-09-18 reorder panel ([`morton.md`](morton.md)).

**The object-dissolve artifact, and why stills cannot show it.** At `tau` above roughly 1.5 (Triton era), a small
persistent object can dissolve partway through a clip and be replaced: about four frames at 24 fps from solid to
gone, and it never recovers. A grid of stills at sampled shot times is unlikely to land on both sides of it; a gate
for it tracks one small persistent object (a hair ornament, jewellery, on-screen text) frame by frame. Forcing the
sensitive blocks or rows exact is cheaper than backing `tau` off globally.

**The earlier judgements, at 124 frames, below the floor.** Four pairs, one observer, one slow-camera fog prompt: one
"Sol better" (the first pair, unblinded), three null. Read as "a speed knob, no quality difference held up", with
severe limits: fatigue pushes toward "the same", and the same observer called one plain-sage render "dramatically
more interesting" than two others differing only by seed.

**Audio.** "Sol-Attn renders are consistently louder" is **retracted**: at 362 frames the two mildest configurations
measured quieter than sage. Audio deviation tracks `tau` (about +1.6 dB mean at 2.0), a change nobody has heard. The
original three pairs, kept for the record:

| pair | seed | mean dB (sage to sol) | peak dB (sage to sol) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 00031/00032 | 1001 | -40.2 to -39.6 (+0.6) | -25.6 to -24.6 (+1.0) |
| 00033/00034 | 1002 | -39.6 to -38.9 (+0.7) | -24.8 to -23.5 (+1.3) |
| 00035/00036 | 1003 | -36.9 to -35.2 (+1.7) | -20.8 to -14.8 (+6.0) |

The renormalization mechanism once written to explain it is an unsupported story. Accuracy against the sage output,
rather than loudness, is still a listening test nobody has done.

**Sage's fp8 against fp16, and Sol's INT8 PV.** The early accuracy ratios were withdrawn and deleted
([`../method/evidence.md`](../method/evidence.md)). The capture-based grade that replaced them is
[`data/results/2026-08-18_sage_accuracy_on_capture.json`](../../data/results/2026-08-18_sage_accuracy_on_capture.json)
(`bench/grade_sage_on_capture.py`, against a float64 reference); read the ratio there. What survives regardless: the
fp8 and fp16 sage kernels differ in both PV operands, so nothing isolates V from P; INT8 V specifically is unmeasured;
and a cosine and an rtol from different harnesses cannot be compared. Sol's INT8 PV is the same class as sage's fp8
arm.

## Record the commit with every measurement

The Triton pack shipped five commits on 2026-08-04 alone, at least two behaviour-changing at long-clip sizes, and the
early timing and audio arms did not record which build ran. On CUDA the branch rebases and a source build declares the
stock version, so put `bench/check_sol_kernel.py`'s output in the run log.
