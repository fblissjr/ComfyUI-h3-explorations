H3 Prompt Standard

What a correct MiniMax H3 prompt looks like, for every mode — and which rules are the vendor's, which are ours, and which are still open. Every rule carries its source, because collapsing that distinction is how two invented rules shipped and were retracted.

01How to read a rule

Every rule below is tagged. The tag tells you what breaking it costs — and whether you may build a checker on it.

Guide · stated

A sentence in MiniMax's own prompt-writing guide says so. Breaking it puts the prompt off-distribution from what the model was trained on. These are the only rules that bind.

Guide · shown

Every vendor example does it; no sentence states it. Follow it, because following the vendor's examples keeps you in-distribution. Do not enforce it, and do not argue from it. Two rules were invented by reading examples as rules, and both were retracted.

Owner

A deliberate decision made on our side. Not the vendor's, not a defect — and it cannot be cited as authority.

House

Our inference from measurement or experience. May itself be wrong. Check before relying on it.

Open

Contradictory, unsourced, or unverified. Do not build a checker on it, and do not quote it as settled.

The single most useful habit: before writing any rule down, ask whether the guide states it or merely shows it. That one question is what separates this document from a style guide someone invented.

02Modes, and the Part One line

Pick the mode first. It decides the field structure, the opening line, and where the style comes from.

ModeYou supplyFormat
T2VAtext onlythree fields
I2VAa first framethree fields
FL2VAfirst and last framethree fields
L2VAa last framethree fields
Ref2VAimages / video / audio referencessix sections

Guide · statedEach keyframe mode has one exact opening line

The guide gives one verbatim string per mode. They differ in bracket use as well as wording, and they are not interchangeable. Resolve Shot N against your final shot and S.SS against the clip's true duration to two decimals. T2VA has no such line at all.

I2VA   For the target video, at 0.00 seconds into the target video,
       <Picture 1> (from [Shot 1]) is fully referenced.

FL2VA  How the reference pictures align with the target video — Picture 1
       (from Shot 1) aligns with the 0.00-second mark of the target video;
       Picture 2 (from Shot N) aligns with the S.SS-second mark of the
       target video.

L2VA   How the reference pictures align with the target video —
       <Picture 1> (from [Shot N]) aligns with the S.SS-second mark of
       the target video.

FL2VA is the only one with no angle brackets and no square brackets. I2VA and L2VA bracket both labels. Getting this wrong is the single most common structural error, and it is silent.

A prompt is correct only at a duration. S.SS and every cut timestamp resolve against the clip's real length, so the same text is right at one length and wrong at another. Fix the frame count before writing.

03Fields, and their order

Guide · statedBase modes: three fields, in this order

integrated_multimodal_description: ...
overall_soundscape: ...
non_diegetic_music: ...

Guide · statedRef2VA: six sections, in this order

subject_definitions: ...
summary: ...
retention_analysis: ...
detailed_description: ...
overall_soundscape: ...
non_diegetic_music: ...

Ref2VA inherits the base format's shot, camera, speaker, dialogue and sound rules by reference, and overrides a small number of things. Never assume a fix that is right for one format is right for the other — they have taken opposite corrections.

Guide · shownMultiple shots run inline, in one unbroken paragraph

Every vendor multi-shot specimen puts [Shot 2] inline rather than on its own line, and a third-party corpus agrees in every specimen that follows the official grammar. No sentence states it, so follow it and do not enforce it. Blank lines go between fields, never inside one.

The exception is Ref2VA's subject_definitions and retention_analysis, where per-line structure is stated — see §10.

04Shots and timing

Guide · statedShot 1 carries no timestamp; later shots do

Later shots open with a strictly increasing cut time: [Shot 2] At 00:03.500, the camera cuts to…. A cut should introduce new information; if only distance or angle changes, prefer camera motion over a cut.

HouseA timestamped cut is the only hard temporal anchor the format has

Nothing else in the prompt tells the model when something happens. If two speakers must share a shot, the ordering has to be carried in prose instead — and prose ordering is weaker than a cut.

OpenHow many spoken words fit in a shot

A budget of roughly 2.5 words per second of shot, minus about a second of air, is commonly used. Its shape is assumed, not measured — a one-parameter form fits the vendor's own example exactly where the two-parameter one is off by a word, and the two diverge by up to 1.8× on short shots, which is where the decisions are tightest. Treat short-shot budgets as possibly far too tight, and never cite either form as measured.

05Speaker IDs — where, how often, per mode

The most-asked operational question, and the guide answers more of it than people expect.

Guide · statedWho gets an ID, and who does not

  • Subjects who speak, sing, or produce an off-screen human voice get a stable ID: (S1), (S2).
  • A speaker keeps the same ID across shots.
  • Characters who never vocalise receive no ID.
  • (S1,S2) means those two speak in unison. Both must already be numbered, so it can never introduce a speaker, and it is never a way to mention a second character.

Guide · statedThe ID goes outside <d>, with the identity and delivery

Verbatim: "Place the speaker's identifying phrase, ID, action, and delivery outside <d>. Inside <d>, include only the language tag and the actual spoken content."

Guide · statedIdentity is established when the speaker first appears

Not when they first speak. Give character type, age, gender, whether they are on-screen, pitch, timbre, speaking rate, accent. Introducing (S2) in one shot and describing them in the next is a violation.

Guide · shownEvery dialogue line names its speaker in the same sentence

Measured across every vendor specimen that contains dialogue: the ID sits in the sentence carrying the <d>, without exception. The vendor never lets a turn run on a bare pronoun, even when the speaker was introduced two sentences earlier.

FormatPattern per speaking shot

Guide · shownOne dialogue turn per shot, in base modes

Every base-format vendor specimen carries exactly one <d> per shot; a large third-party corpus does the same in every dialogue-carrying shot without exception. In Ref2VA the vendor is split — its own reference payload puts two turns in one shot — so two is attested there and stacking beyond that is not.

06Dialogue blocks and markers

Guide · statedThe <d> block, and its language tag

The woman with the low measured voice (S1) says:
<d>[English] You said tomorrow.</d>

Inside the block: the language tag and the spoken words only. Preserve every original word and punctuation mark verbatim — do not translate or rewrite. Everything else — who is speaking, their ID, the action, the delivery — goes outside.

HouseSeven special tokens exist; the guides describe only two

The released tokenizer declares <d>, </d>, <|cutoff|>, <|lyrics_start|>, <|lyrics_end|>, <|caption_start|>, <|caption_end|>. The guides document only the dialogue pair. Every structural rule about the other five is house pattern, not vendor guidance.

Watch the spelling. Both guides print <cutoff> unpiped, and the release declares only the piped <|cutoff|>. Write the piped form — that is a deliberate divergence from the guide's prose in favour of the token that actually exists. The guides also mention <scenetrans>, which matches no declared token at all; leave it as ordinary prose.

Guide · statedOn-screen text is typed literally, in double quotes

Any banner, sign, label, subtitle or neon text visible on screen goes in English double quotation marks, verbatim and untranslated. Describe text without giving the string and the model draws the texture of writing rather than the writing — plausible-looking letterforms that spell nothing.

HouseClose the mouth when the shot continues past the line

The pattern in the corpora that have been counted: a mouth-closing cue follows a dialogue line when the shot continues, and never when the line ends the shot — a cut or the field end does that job instead. The vendor corpus does not corroborate this; it comes from a third-party corpus. The only stated vendor rule here is narrower: after a voiceover block, state that the on-screen character's lips remain closed.

HouseSilent characters must be marked silent

Every on-screen character not given an explicit "produces no vocal sound" is a candidate for the model to voice anyway.

07The style opening

Guide · statedState the style and initial composition at the start of [Shot 1]

This is a requirement, not a flourish. The guide's own wording: "At the beginning of [Shot 1], state the overall style and initial composition."

The style list is open, not closed. The guide says "common styles include" and names Cinematic, live-action, 2D-animated, 3D CG, claymation, watercolor, vintage film. Other styles are legal; these are the attested ones.

Where the style comes from differs by mode — also stated: for keyframe tasks, derive it from the reference image; for T2VA, take it from the user's text.

[Shot 1] Live-action, cinematic, a medium-wide shot frames…

08Camera motion

Guide · statedMotion type + amplitude + speed, written as natural English

Written inside the shot as an action, not stacked as labels at the end of a sentence. Add amplitude and speed only when meaningful — medium amplitude and normal speed are usually omitted.

The camera pushes in with small amplitude at slow speed toward the folded letter.

Note the distinction the table draws and that is easy to blur: Truck Left/Right is a horizontal translation; Tracking Shot is following a moving subject. They are separate rows.

OwnerStandard cinematography vocabulary is acceptable

Terms outside the table are fine so long as they align with common cinematography — a dolly move described as a dolly move is not an error. The table is the attested vocabulary, not a closed set to police. Revisit only if output quality gives a reason to.

HouseThe model moves the camera unless told otherwise

Say nothing and expect a slow drift. Reported to be more reliably fixed by naming the moves it should not make than by asking for a static shot. Not measured here.

09The two audio fields

Guide · statedoverall_soundscape

One to four English sentences, in one continuous paragraph: ambient sound, physical action sounds, non-verbal human sounds. Dialogue, singing and diegetic music do not belong here — they are already in the description and must not be repeated.

Guide · statednon_diegetic_music

One to three sentences on instrumentation, speed, rhythm and dynamic change. Not mood words, and not the emotional function of the score. Music the characters can hear — singing, radio, TV, a phone — is diegetic and belongs in the description instead.

Write N/A when there is no non-diegetic music. Note the condition: it is a statement about the scene, not a default. Reaching for N/A by habit asserts that every video you make is unscored.

10Reference labels (Ref2VA only)

Guide · statedOne line per item, in two of the six sections

subject_definitions gives each item its own line, explaining what its label denotes, its reference role, and the features to follow. retention_analysis uses one line per reference label.

The exception that catches people: if a <Picture N> or <Video N> only identifies the source of another item and is never analysed separately, cite it inside that item's definition rather than giving it its own line.

<Subject 1> is the main character in <Picture 1>, whose face, hair and
clothing are carried into the target video.
<Subject 2> is the environment in <Picture 2>, which provides the setting.
<Audio 1> is the voice-timbre reference for <Subject 1> (S1).

Guide · statedLabels are used in the body, and the body is written in English

Reference labels appear in detailed_description and stay consistent across all sections. Write the body in English; preserve the original language only for dialogue and lyrics inside <d>, and for text visibly present in the scene.

Guide · statedLength: 350–500 words for generation tasks

Dialogue-dense content prioritises fitting the complete spoken timeline over hitting a word count, and editing descriptions scale with source complexity instead. A single shot does not justify a short description — distribute detail across shots by information load.

HouseOrdinals follow the order items are built, and soundtracks jump the queue

Counters are per kind and independent, so the same file can be <Video 1> and <Audio 2>. A reference video's soundtrack takes its <Audio> ordinal before its own <Video> label, and standalone audio is numbered after. One soundtracked video plus one standalone clip gives <Audio 1> = the soundtrack.

11What good looks like

Both graded clean against the guide's mechanical rules. Each is written for a stated duration.

T2VA · 243 frames · 10.125 s · two shots 
integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] Live-action, cinematic, a medium
shot frames a night ferry deck across the ten-second take, wet steel railing
in the foreground and harbour lights smeared behind. A dock worker in her
forties with a low, level alto (S1) leans on the rail, on-screen, unhurried
delivery, and says: <d>[English] Last crossing until Thursday.</d> Her lips
close and her jaw stops moving as she pushes back from the rail. A younger man
in a canvas jacket stands two paces behind her and produces no vocal sound.
The camera trucks right with small amplitude at slow speed, carrying the
harbour lights across the frame. [Shot 2] At 00:05.000, the shot cuts to a
close shot of the man's hands folding a paper timetable against his knee. The
man, on-screen, mid-twenties, with a dry, slightly hoarse tenor (S2), looks up
and answers: <d>[English] Then we wait it out here.</d> His lips settle closed
and the paper flattens under his thumb.

overall_soundscape: Diesel engine rumble carries under a steady wash of water
against the hull. Wind pulls at loose canvas, boots scuff on wet steel, and a
mooring chain knocks twice against the deck plate.

non_diegetic_music: A single sustained low synth tone at a slow tempo, joined
by a soft brushed-snare pulse that rises briefly and drops away before the
final frame.

Why it works: style opens Shot 1; each speaker is introduced with identity, on-screen status and voice quality at the moment they first appear; the ID sits in the sentence carrying the line; one turn per shot; the non-speaker is explicitly silent; the cut carries a timestamp and Shot 1 does not; the camera phrase is type + amplitude + speed; the soundscape carries no dialogue.

I2VA · 345 frames · 14.375 s · one shot 
For the target video, at 0.00 seconds into the target video, <Picture 1>
(from [Shot 1]) is fully referenced.

integrated_multimodal_description: [Shot 1] Live-action, cinematic, holding the
exact framing, lighting, wardrobe and composition established in <Picture 1>, a
bench joiner in a canvas apron stands over a half-planed board in a timber
workshop, low sun coming through sawdust in the air behind her. She sets her
weight, draws the plane the length of the board, and a single curled shaving
lifts and falls to the floor. She produces no vocal sound. The camera pulls out
with small amplitude at slow speed, opening the frame to the racked chisels
along the back wall.

overall_soundscape: The long dry rasp of a hand plane on softwood repeats at an
unhurried pace, each pass ending in the light tick of a shaving dropping to
boards. Room tone is close and wooden, with a faint hum from a strip light
overhead.

non_diegetic_music: N/A

Why it works: the Part One line is the I2VA string exactly, brackets included; the opening clause holds the picture's framing and lighting before any new action; the only character on screen is explicitly silent; one continuous shot needs no timestamp.

12What bad looks like

Every one of these was found in real shipped prompts, ours included.

Wrong mode's Part One line

Using the I2VA sentence on an FL2VA prompt, or leaving Shot N and S.SS unresolved. Silent — the render succeeds and the alignment is simply wrong. Resolve both against the actual final shot and the actual snapped duration.

A turn on a bare pronoun 
He scoops once, lets the bolts run back into the bin, and answers:
<d>[English] The next size up.</d>

The speaker was introduced with (S1) earlier in the shot, and this turn carries no ID. No vendor specimen ever does this. Repeat the ID on the speaking sentence.

A subject label without its ID 
<Subject 1> calls after him already reading the next ticket:
<d>[English] Where is my second plate.</d>

In Ref2VA the ID binds to the label — <Subject 1> (S1) — every time it is used in a speaking sentence, not just at the definition.

A compound ID used to mention someone 
The purple rabbit (S4,S5), a male-presenting creature … says: <d>…</d>

(S4,S5) instructs two characters to say the line in unison. It is not shorthand for "and this other character is here". It also cannot introduce a speaker, since both IDs must already exist.

Naming a character instead of describing them

A bare proper noun gives the model nothing to draw — it renders what is described, not what is named, and will invent a plausible-adjacent character and then render that. Expand every name once into a canonical visual noun phrase plus a voice description, and reuse that phrase verbatim on every mention. Keep the name too; never rely on it.

Describing on-screen text instead of typing it

"HUD elements" renders as letterform-shaped noise. Give the literal string in double quotes.

Dialogue in the soundscape

overall_soundscape is ambience, physical action and non-verbal human sound. Speech, singing and diegetic music belong in the description and must not be repeated here.

Timing written by feel

Nothing in the prompt tells the writer how long the clip is — duration is a generation parameter, not a prompt field. Cut timestamps picked by vibe overrun the clip, and the last beat is the one that gets squeezed. Fix the frame count first, then write to it.

Reaching for N/A by habit

Legal, and it means "this video has no non-diegetic music". Writing it into every prompt is a creative decision made by default rather than on purpose.
