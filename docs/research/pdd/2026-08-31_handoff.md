# 2026-08-31 — what merging a PDD LoRA onto int8 costs, and what still is not known

**Read this before acting on anything about `unmerged_blocks`.** It became the
lane's leading lever today on evidence that is entirely STORED-WEIGHT. Nothing
here was rendered. Whether any of it is visible in output is the open question,
not a detail.

Three lanes worked this: PDD (`quant_levers.md`, the lever inventory),
Sol/VSA ([`../merge_requantisation.md`](../merge_requantisation.md), the data
and provenance record), and Codex (reviews, departed mid-afternoon). This file
is the PDD-facing summary; neither of those two is restated here and both
outrank this one on their own subject.

## 1. The finding

A LoRA merged onto an `int8_convrot` module is **dequantised, added and
requantised**. Our node does that to the BACKBONE — `pdd_lora.py:1881`,
`m.add_patches(loaded, strength)`, ComfyUI's native weight-patch path — and
does NOT do it to the output heads, which go through `add_object_patch` and are
applied at the call. **That split is the most important fact here**: PDD's
replicated heads, which are what PDD *is*, are untouched.

Requantisation on the shipped path uses **stochastic rounding**, and
round-to-nearest is unreachable: over 466 module weight keys
`comfy.utils.string_to_seed` returns a minimum of 12,054,335 and none returns
0, so `_round_int8` always takes the stochastic branch. Executed, not inferred.

Measured over **all 200 modules** of each arm, 50 blocks x 4 kinds:

| arm | delta/step | realised | noise/&#124;d&#124; median | min | max | noise > update |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| PDD fl2va (ships) | 0.07557 | 1.0000 | 1.981 | 0.274 | 3.121 | 194/200 (97%) |
| PDD ref2va (ships) | 0.08284 | 1.0000 | 1.848 | 0.239 | 3.209 | 193/200 (96%) |
| turbo fl2v (contrast) | 0.00232 | 0.9991 | 12.156 | 3.107 | 27.313 | 200/200 |

So: **the update lands** (`realised` ~1.0 — stochastic rounding is unbiased),
and on nearly every module the requantisation perturbs the weights by MORE than
the update it is delivering. The driving variable is the delta's size against
one quantisation step, not the LoRA's provenance; PDD and turbo sit at
different points on one continuous curve, 16x apart because PDD's `alpha/rank`
is 1.0 and turbo's is 0.0625.

## 2. What is closed

- **Which PDD file does not matter.** `adaln2688` and the shipped conversion
  agree on every summary statistic to the digit across 200 modules.
- **Which base does not matter.** Pruned and unpruned likewise. Both follow
  from the same fact: only the backbone merges, and the variants differ in the
  adaln/head payload that does not.
- **VSA is out of scope** as shipped — a baked checkpoint, 0 `lora_*` keys,
  never merged. A future VSA shipping as a LoRA inherits all of this.
- **The subsampling question.** See §5.

## 3. What is NOT closed, in priority order

1. **Whether any of it is visible at the output.** Nothing was rendered. The
   noise is unbiased and random-direction, and PDD demonstrably produces
   working video. This is a cost of known magnitude and unknown effect.
2. ~~The proportion question.~~ **Closed, and see §6 — it changed the
   headline.**
3. **`unmerged_blocks` at the output.** It sidesteps requantisation entirely.
   Its per-artifact ordering is denominator-dependent (§6) and its value at
   the output is unmeasured. **The DEFAULT, however, does not need Tier 1** —
   see §6's last paragraph.
4. **The per-kind ordering is partition-dependent** and only fl2va is measured
   in both lanes. fl2va runs `fc2` > `fc1` > `out_proj` > `qkv`; ref2va runs
   `out_proj` > `fc2` > `fc1` > `qkv`. Only `qkv_proj` being least affected is
   common. **Do not carry a kind ordering across the partition boundary.**

## 4. Tier 1 observer: built, green, wired into nothing

Eight acceptance cases, no card time spent. Codex reviewed it and reported four
defects before leaving without writing its note; they were preserved at
`internal/codex/2026-08-31_observer-review-findings-RELAYED.md` and **all four
reproduced**. Two were already fixed; two were real and one would have killed
the first capture:

- **Step identity was mis-scaled.** `timestep` is 0..1000
  (`comfy/ldm/minimax/model.py:561` divides by 1000) and `sample_sigmas` are
  0..1, so an argmin compared ~1000 against ~1.0 and every row carried the same
  step label — while the record looked complete.
- **The fc2 seam materialised 2.79 GiB** on a 24 GiB card already holding a
  19.5 GiB checkpoint. The observer's docstring said "no full-tensor `abs()` or
  `float()`", which was TRUE of the function carrying it and FALSE one frame
  up in the MLP wrapper. Now chunked by BYTES rather than rows, peak 193–226
  MiB at production geometry.
- The composition patch **neither chained nor refused on
  `diffusion_model.forward`**, the one key PDD also patches, so it would have
  silently evicted PDD's step tracker on any PDD graph.

**The gate did its job.** None of that reached a render.

## 5. Method lessons the day earned, each with its instance

- **A metric that ranks two arms is a claim about the metric.** Three
  statistics here rank the arms differently: stored-weight distance prefers the
  arm that does nothing, `realised` prefers the arm that adds noise, and only
  `noise/|d|` sees the third failure. Two lanes each published a conclusion
  from a metric blind to the next one, in consecutive commits.
- **A subsample preserves the statistics people quote and loses the ones that
  bound the claim.** Measured: a 100-module half of the fl2va arm gave median
  1.979 against the full 1.981 — the centre to three decimals — but minimum
  0.877 against the true **0.274**, and 99% against 97%. The loss was
  STRUCTURED, not random: the two kinds easiest to sample were `qkv_proj` and
  `fc2`, and `qkv_proj` is the least affected on both partitions. Ask what
  makes a module easy to sample before trusting a subset of them.
- **An assertion is scoped to what carries it.** The 2.79 GiB case above is the
  sharpest instance: a docstring truthfully denying materialisation, one frame
  below the code doing it.
- **Reading a name is not reading a fact.** A field called
  `reproduced_from_bf16` held a COMPUTE dtype and was read as a source dtype,
  inventing an encoder/DiT divergence. Separately, a `*bf16*` filename search
  missed the sharded release DiT and produced a flat "there is no BF16 H3 DiT
  on this box", which is false.
- **Second implementations, not second readings.** Every correction today came
  from someone re-deriving a number independently — never from anyone
  re-reading their own work. Two lanes deliberately keep separate
  implementations of the same statistic (`measure_merge_noise.py` and
  `measure_merge_realisation.py`) for exactly this reason. Do not collapse them.

## 6. The headline changed twice at the end of the day. Quote the third one.

Everything above was first quoted against the DELTA. Two corrections followed,
in this order, and the second supersedes the first:

**First**, against the WEIGHT the ranking REVERSES. Same 200 modules:
PDD fl2va **0.921%** (worst 1.240%), PDD ref2va **0.947%**, turbo **0.172%**
(worst 0.495%). Turbo looks 6x worse than PDD against the delta and is 5x
better against the weight, because its delta is 34x smaller relative to the
weight — twelve times a tiny thing is still tiny. That withdrew "unmerged_blocks
matters most on turbo", which both lanes had concluded from `noise/|d|` alone.

**Second, and this is the one to quote**: the reference point is what the
checkpoint ALREADY carries. Base int8 error against the BF16 release, median
**0.910%** of the weight over 200 modules (range 0.881–1.256%). So:

    PDD merge noise    0.921% of the weight
    base int8 error    0.910% of the weight   <- already there, no LoRA involved

The merge injects an error term **about the size of the entire int8
quantisation**, which against the release is the **+11.6%** on total
stored-weight error the lever inventory reports (0.936% -> 1.045%).

**Three true sentences about one fact** — "twice the update", "0.92% of the
weight", "+11.6% on the total" — reading as alarming, negligible and modest.
**Quote the +11.6% against the release.** It answers "how much worse is the
merged model", which is what a reader is asking. The 2x answers "how faithfully
is the update delivered", which is narrower and is what both lanes led with all
day. The reference point is the PDD lane's, produced in its final message.

> +11.6% is wrong — a second implementation reproduces it BIT-IDENTICALLY —

> *"deterministic; the shipped path uses seeded stochastic rounding, so these

> `E[Q_s(x)] = x` is exactly why §2b withdrew the deterministic-merge lever;
> `E‖Q_s(x) − x‖ > ‖Q_rtn(x) − x‖` is the √2 the same day measured. A distance
> statistic cannot borrow the first identity.
>
> Measured over the same 200 modules
> (`bench/measure_merge_rounding_regimes.py`,
> [`../../../bench/results/2026-08-31_merge_rounding_regimes.json`](../../../bench/results/2026-08-31_merge_rounding_regimes.json)):

## 7. Decision taken 2026-08-31: HOLD on the Tier 1 gate

Not a pause for lack of time — a judgement, and the reasons are on the record:

- **The observer's record format moved three times today**, each time from
  someone else's review rather than its author's re-reading. Building
  `bench/grade_quant_on_capture.py` now means building against a schema still
  in motion.
- **The corrections have not converged.** Eight or nine rounds across three
  lanes, and the last one was inside the summary meant to settle it. Every
  round improved the result; none came from anyone re-reading their own work.
  That rate is a signal the lane is still moving.
- **Gate item 5 is a DECISION, not a task.** Whether one `capture_id` is
  published to both instruments or the join is documented on `(block, step)`
  has to be chosen, not discovered. Choosing wrong produces two plausible files
  with different counters, which is the exact failure the contract exists to
  prevent. Five minutes, and not to be made in passing by whoever builds first.

**What would unlock it**: wanting the render soon (then gate items 1–4 are the
critical path and are mechanical), or another lane needing a capture for its
own question (then the joint graph is worth building for them). Absent one of
those, the highest-value output of the day is the corrected framing in §6, and
the second-highest is that **nothing was rendered under four defects that all
reproduced**.

**The hold survives the §6 correction.** A bigger number does not make the
observer's schema less in motion, and the correction arrived from a fifth round
of review, which is the signal §7 is reading rather than an argument against
it.

## 9. What tomorrow starts from

Written the evening of 2026-08-31 so the next session does not re-derive the
ordering. **Nothing below has been rendered and none of it needs the card to
start.**

**The lever ordering changed, and the reason is not the size of a number.**
`unmerged_blocks` was the leading lever because the bake was believed to cost
a second artifact per partition. It does not: a bake pins `strength` and
nothing else, because the partition never reaches the backbone
([`../../h3_pdd.md`](../../h3_pdd.md), "What a backbone bake pins"). With that
gone the bake wins on fidelity, on runtime cost, on module coverage, and on the
delivery statistic — and `unmerged_blocks` keeps two advantages the fidelity
table does not show: it needs no new artifact and no provenance surface, and it
is the only one of the two that also bypasses the ACTIVATION rounding on the
delta.

| | modules reached | stored-weight error | run-time cost | delivery | new artifact |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| merged (ships) | 200 | 1.4046x base | none | 1.0000 | no |
| `unmerged_blocks=0-49` | **150** — `fc2` unreachable | 1.0236x base | +2.4% FLOPs (untimed) | exact | no |
| offline bake | **200** | **1.0000019x base** | **none** | 1.0000 | yes, plus a strength contract |

**Corrected 2026-09-02:** the `1.0236x` cell combined the three-kind reach
fraction with the deterministic RTN gap. That is not the merge the shipped
node replaces. Re-aggregating the full 200-module seeded-stochastic record
through `pdd_lora.py`'s literal `UNMERGED_KINDS` gives **1.096307x base**:
the 150 reachable modules return to their checkpoint error while all 50
`mlp.fc2` modules remain stochastically merged. This recovers **76.2%** of the
shipped merge gap. The corrected executable aggregation is
[`../../../bench/analyze_pdd_unmerge_curve.py`](../../../bench/analyze_pdd_unmerge_curve.py)
and its [record](../../../bench/results/2026-08-31_pdd_unmerge_recovery.json).

The 150 is not a scope choice: `mlp.fc2.forward` is never called on the INT8
path, so it cannot take a forward patch
([`../../../pdd_lora.py`](../../../pdd_lora.py), `UNMERGED_KINDS`). The three
reachable kinds are **79.8%** of the deterministic RTN merge gap and **76.2%**
of the shipped seeded-stochastic gap. Neither share says
nothing about network sensitivity — `blocks.49.mlp.fc2` carries the largest
single update in the file.

**In order, and each one earns the next:**

1. **Decide the bake's artifact contract before writing a converter.** Two
   choices, both in [`../../h3_pdd.md`](../../h3_pdd.md): stripped sidecar
   against node-side detection, and how a baked strength is declared and
   refused. Gate item 5's lesson — a decision, not a task, and choosing wrong
   produces two plausible artifacts nobody can tell apart. Minutes, no card.
2. **Two pieces of tooling, and neither is "a converter" from scratch.**
   [`../../../bench/convert_pdd_lora.py`](../../../bench/convert_pdd_lora.py)
   already owns the artifact FORMAT and already takes `--base`; the sidecar for
   a baked checkpoint is a filter on what it emits (`--omit-backbone`, or
   whatever the contract in step 1 settles), which keeps one authority for the
   format instead of two. The bake itself is a second script, because it writes
   a 19.5 GB DiT rather than a LoRA — and every piece of it exists:
   `bench/measure_merge_rounding_regimes.py::requant_kitchen` is the shipped
   quantisation reproduced to the format floor, `bench/analyze_quant_delta.py::hf_to_comfy`
   maps the release keys, `bench/build_hybrid.py` is the template for writing a
   modified int8 checkpoint, and the per-module layout is `weight` I8 +
   `weight_scale` F32 + `comfy_quant` copied through unchanged. CPU only, and
   1.3 TB free. **Assert 200 modules written, not 50 blocks** — the
   `_shape_check` rule, and this lane has already shipped one artifact that
   dropped a kind silently.
   **Added 2026-09-02: the sidecar filter is not the whole change.**
   `pdd_lora.py` raises "matched no module" when `comfy.lora.load_lora`
   returns empty for the backbone, so a stripped sidecar is refused as a
   stale conversion before anything else runs. The guard has to move to
   asserting the 8 refiner modules matched;
   [`../../h3_pdd.md`](../../h3_pdd.md)'s stripped-sidecar bullet carries
   the correction and the tensor counts to assert against.
3. **Then the first render is merged against baked**, which is a complete
   200-module intervention with no forward patching, no Sol interaction to
   reason about, and no runtime confound. `h3_text_to_video_pdd` already ships
   at **8 evaluations** and 8 is the trained width — 4 sits at the outer edge
   of the `[trained, 2*trained]` envelope, where PDD's own coarseness is the
   documented first-order effect and would mask this one.
4. **`unmerged_blocks` is the third arm, not the first.** Against the bake it
   isolates the activation rounding on the delta; against merged it confounds
   that with the weight-grid term. Running it first cannot separate them, which
   is the ambiguity nothing in this lane currently resolves.

**Two things that are NOT available, so do not plan on them.**

- **There is no runnable BF16 H3 DiT.** The release is here — 62 GB of
  diffusers-format shards under `{FL2VA,Ref2VA}/transformer` — and no
  comfy-layout BF16 DiT exists anywhere on this box (every H3 file in
  `models/diffusion_models` is int8, fp8 or w4a8). A BF16 reference arm needs a
  62 GB conversion plus a streaming load on a 24 GB card. That is a build, not
  a measurement, and any plan whose headline is a ratio against BF16 is
  planning a build day.
- **A same-seed pair of renders cannot A/B this.** `CLAUDE.md`'s different-sample
  rule: two arms differing in a weight diverge completely, so the output is a
  different sample rather than a degraded one. The comparison is "does each arm
  meet the brief", judged blind over a distribution
  ([`../../eval_comparison.md`](../../eval_comparison.md) §3), and one pair is
  a screen.

## Addendum 2026-09-01: the Sol interaction is observable now, so record it

Item 3 above says the merged-against-baked render has "no Sol interaction to
reason about". Since 2026-09-01 it does not need reasoning about: the Sol
route record (`sol_observe.py`, `H3_SOL_OBSERVE`, `bench/grade_sol_record.py`,
`bench/compare_sol_records.py`) reports every attention call's route and
routed density per block and step, and a bake that changes Q/K can change
routing. So render BOTH arms armed and compare their counts call for call
before attributing anything to the bake. The control that makes that
comparison meaningful already ran: the shipped PDD graph rendered cold and
warm, across a restart, gave bitwise identical counts on all 184 Sol calls
(`bench/results/2026-09-01_sol_route_pdd8_cold_vs_warm.json`), so a
difference between merged and baked arms is the arms', not the cache's.
Timings from an armed render are not quotable; use it for routing only.
