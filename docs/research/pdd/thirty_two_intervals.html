Thirty-Two Intervals

PDD's trajectory is always 32 intervals. It does not get shorter when you ask for fewer steps. What the step count decides is how those 32 intervals are grouped into blocks — and how many times the network actually runs.

NFE is not the same thing as steps

NFE means number of function evaluations: how many times the network actually runs during sampling. It is the honest cost unit, because a "step" does not always cost one forward pass.

first-order sampler (euler), no CFGNFE = steps
second-order sampler (heun, dpmpp_2m)NFE = 2 × steps
CFG ondoubles again

Every graph here runs euler with no CFG, so NFE and steps are the same number. That is not a coincidence — it is why the node can read the schedule directly and get the blocks right without reconciling two different counts.

PDD needs the distinction anyway, because its trajectory is 32 points regardless of how many times you evaluate. Calling an 8-evaluation render "8 steps" would collide with the 32 the trajectory actually has. "8 NFE over a 32-point grid" says it without ambiguity.

The grid never moves. The blocks do.

The output head is replicated once per interval of that 32-point grid. A sampling step fuses a contiguous run of those heads into one effective linear whose output is that block's mean velocity, and a single Euler step crosses the whole block.

What one render actually does

  1. The 32-interval grid is fixed by the converted file and never changes.
  2. The sampler builds its sigma schedule from the step count you set.
  3. The node maps each of those sigmas back to a grid index. Those indices become the block boundaries.
  4. Each forward decodes one block, using a head that is that block's dt-weighted mean velocity.
  5. One Euler step carries the latent across the entire block to the next boundary.

8 is the nominal arm — it is what the file is named for and what the vendor's README reports. 4 is the other count they report. 2 and 16 are legal arithmetic rather than sensible arms: 2 averages sixteen intervals into a single velocity, and 16 buys nothing over 8.

5 and 6 still work. They just do not tile.

A count that divides 32 gives blocks of equal width. One that does not still renders — the node takes whatever spans the sampler asks for — but the blocks come out ragged, and a partial block is off the distribution the heads were distilled on. The node logs that it happened rather than refusing.

The last step swallows the render

This is the part that surprises people. The shifted schedule is heavily back-loaded: the final evaluation covers most of the distance from noise to image, and the fewer steps you take the more it dominates.

Two things are shrinking at once

As the step count falls, the final step takes on more of the work — and it is also the step where PDD's fused heads deviate most from the base head. At the same time Sol-Attn's share of the render drops, from 11 of 16 steps to 2 of 4, because its window is a fixed sigma band rather than a fraction of the steps.

So PDD and Sol are buying time from overlapping budgets. Halving the steps does not halve the render and then let Sol halve it again.

What does not change with the step count

Only the sampler's steps moves. Not the sigma shift, which stays at the checkpoint's own 12 video / 3 audio. Not the scheduler. Not the converted file, which serves every count from one 32-interval bank. Not the LoRA.

That holds because of an exact identity, not an approximation: subsampling the 32-point grid every block intervals lands precisely on the plain shifted schedule for that step count. Verified to bit equality at every divisor:

pdd_time_grid(12.0, 32)[::16] == pdd_time_grid(12.0,  2)   True
pdd_time_grid(12.0, 32)[::8]  == pdd_time_grid(12.0,  4)   True
pdd_time_grid(12.0, 32)[::4]  == pdd_time_grid(12.0,  8)   True
pdd_time_grid(12.0, 32)[::2]  == pdd_time_grid(12.0, 16)   True

The model is evaluated at exactly the times a normal render of that step count would use. This is why a PDD arm needs no special scheduler and no sigma-shift change.

The one thing you must move with it

Sol-Attn's end_percent. Its window is a sigma band, so which steps land inside it depends on the count, and the node resolves percent to sigma at patch time when the step count is not yet knowable. The generator derives it per count instead. Open a shipped graph, change steps by hand, and end_percent goes stale with nothing at run time to say so.
