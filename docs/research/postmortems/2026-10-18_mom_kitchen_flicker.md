## 1. Starting from the 2026-10-15 Postmortem: What It Got Right and What Came After

### What [`2026-10-15_span_fun-kitchen-and-whole-shot.md`](2026-10-15_span_fun-kitchen-and-whole-shot.md) got right

On 2026-10-15, the assembled kitchen cut (`Video/fun/day_2026-10-15/shots/fun_0604_1009_joined_2026-10-15T2110Z.mp4`) covered the 406-frame kitchen shot (`604–1009`) with two separate loads of the second subject (`second_hfp_0604d` on `604–948` and `second_hfp_0949d` on `949–998`) and left the final 11 frames (`999–1009`) unrendered after `second_hfp_0949` (`949–1009`) and `second_hfp_0998`/`0998b` (`998–1009`) failed their gates on the lead's false tracker mask on `1003–1009`. The 2026-10-15 postmortem accurately identified five structural causes of the failures visible at that point:

1. **Splitting a continuous shot into separate loads (`604–948` + `949–998` + unrendered `999–1009`)**: Starting `second_hfp_0949d` cold at frame `949` with zero latent context from `604–948` forced the model to re-solve the second subject's facial geometry, hair parting, hair length (`hair_band: 0.6` on `0604d` vs no `hair_band` on `0949d`), and warm tungsten lighting from `<Picture 1>` alone, producing a hard 1-frame identity, pose, and hair jump across `948 -> 949` and an 11-frame reversion to the dark-haired original across `999–1009`.
2. **Whole-shot one-load rule and seam-grid chaining**: Established that a subject in a shot is rendered as one load (`<= 362` frames in one window; `> 362` frames in chained windows picked by `window_seams` with `context_noise: 0.8`), giving `604–1009` two windows `[294, 243]` (`604–897` and `808–1009`, writing `898–1009` with 90 kept context frames `808–897` at noise `0.8`).
3. **Bounding the part mask by the subject's own track (`part_on_track`, `parts_held_to_track_px: 8`) and `strangers` keep-out**: Sapiens's 12-px halo around `second`'s track (`shot_c_0604b_second_parts_00001.mkv`, used by `0604d`) bled onto the lead's hair, face, and shoulder when the lead stood beside or behind her (`687–773`, `851–948`), and the figure on the cereal box (`strangers`) needed to be kept out of `second`'s 64-px margin. Emptying the lead's false tracker mask on `1003–1009` (`--track-not`, `shot_c_0604b_lead_track_not_1003-1009.mkv`) unblocked the full `604–1009` span.
4. **Reference conditioning for shut eyes (`--eyes-shut-on`) and mouth gating (`CLASS_MOUTH_GATE`)**: On `938–953` and `968–1009`, the second subject's eyes are shut in the source, but `<Video 1>` in `0604d` and `0949d` drew open eye outlines with dark irises inside them (`43` of `50` frames of `0949d`), causing `0949d` to snap her eyes wide open at `949`. Adding `--eyes-shut-on 938-953,968-1009 --eyes-shut-form lids` drew single closed lid lines on those spans, while `CLASS_MOUTH_GATE` (`0.5` of lip segments on Sapiens lip/teeth/tongue classes) kept the mouth outline on `323` of `406` frames (`no_mouth_on` on `32` frames where her hand or head turn occluded the mouth) instead of dropping the mouth across the whole clip as `0949d` had done.
5. **Prompting as a whole video rather than an edit (`load_text.NO_SCENE`, `hair_change`)**: Removed the scene-and-position clause (`"sits at a kitchen table"`) and changed her hair description from `"long, loose blonde hair parted in the middle"` (which fought her short-haired head turns and apron straps) to `"short blonde hair ending at her shoulders"` (`retention_analysis: partially_preserved` with length changed to short).

### What came after (`2026-10-15` night through `2026-10-17`)

On the night of 2026-10-15 (`23:57Z`), `mrgo` queued `second_hfp_0604g` (`604–1009`, 406 frames) with all five of the 2026-10-15 postmortem's fixes applied. `second_hfp_0604g` succeeded in eliminating the `948/949` and `998/999` load joins and the `949` open-eye pop, **but it still did not replace `0604d` + `0949d` across the whole shot** (in the `2026-10-16` and `2026-10-17T1747Z` full-length cuts, `0604d` was still laid on `604–948`, `0949d` on `949–998`, and `0604g` only on the 11-frame tail `999–1009`).

Inspection and measurements across 2026-10-16 and 2026-10-17 (`mrtoon`, `mrtony`, `mrgemsol`, `mrtabby`, `mrted`) uncovered four remaining mechanisms — three of which the 2026-10-15 postmortem had left untouched or treated as helpful keep-outs — that continued to punch holes into the second subject's hair, neck, and face or starve her region of shoulder room:

1. **Assembly-time `restore=` masks on the replaced subject's own parts and `assemble_delivery.py`'s `change_threshold` (`2026-10-16`, `mrtoon`)**: Up through `fun_0604_1009_joined_2026-10-15T2110Z.mp4` and the morning cuts of `2026-10-16`, `data/fun/plan.json` carried `restore=second.Left_Hand+Right_Hand+Left_Lower_Arm+Right_Lower_Arm+Left_Upper_Arm+Right_Upper_Arm` and `restore=second.Apparel@head`, and `bench/assemble_delivery.py` recomputed a per-pixel difference matte (`diff > 0.04`). 2. **`pieces="largest"` on the subject's part mask (`2026-10-16`, `mrtoon` / `mrtony`)**: Both `0604d` (`286/345` frames) and `0604g` (`304/406` frames) ran with `pieces="largest"` on `second`'s part mask (`Face_Neck + Hair`). Because `video_mask.py` only protects `r["mask"]` from keep-outs, dropping her lower-neck island allowed the keep-outs to carve those cells out of the token region entirely (`220` of `406` frames in `0604g` had true `part_on_track` pixels left unregenerated outside `tokens`).
3. **`composite="only what changed"` (`change_threshold=0.05` in the song node, `2026-10-16` / `2026-10-17` N1, `mrtoon` / `mrgemsol` / `mrtabby`)**: Both `0604d`/`0949d` and `0604g` were laid by the song node with `composite="only what changed"`. Inside the regenerated margin (`tok & ~r["mask"]`, plus any `part` islands dropped by `pieces="largest"`), every pixel where the generated blonde hair, fair skin, or apron strap sat within `0.05` (`12.75 / 255` mean RGB) of the warm amber kitchen wall, sunlit curtain, or white apron was restored to the source (`22.24%` of `0604d`'s token region, `16.41%` of `0949d`'s, and `21.40%` of `0604g`'s), producing frame-to-frame threshold mottling on her neck and cheek (`644–675`, `676–747`, `808–948`).
4. **Self-keep-outs `held`, `hands`, `clothes`/`clothing`, and `hair_band` (`2026-10-16` / `2026-10-17` N2, `r16n_06`, `mrgemsol` / `mrtabby`)**: Even when `0604g` was re-laid with `composite="whole region"` (`second_hfp_0604g_whole_region`), its token region had already been carved during sampling by `held`, `hands`, and `clothing` (`Apparel + Upper_Clothing + Lower_Clothing + Torso`). Because `0604g` retired `hair_band: 0.6` while keeping `clothing` in `others`, the `clothing`, `hands`, and `held` keep-outs removed **`9,622 px/frame`** (`1.22%` of the canvas on `406/406` frames) from her 64-px grown margin right up to her jawline and collar. With zero room below her jaw to draw shoulder-length hair or a connected neck/collar, `0604g` was forced to draw a short cropped skull-cap above the frozen original neck and apron straps (`604–637`, `676–747`, `752–773`, `780–807`) and stepped 16x16 latent-cell cutouts on her throat (`808–948`). Retiring `held`, `hands`, `clothes`/`clothing`, and `hair_band` in `second_hfp_0604h` (and naming her apron and floral blouse in the prompt) let the 64-px margin cover her neck, shoulders, and upper apron bib (`17.90%` of the frame vs `14.52%` in `0604g`) as one unbitten region.

### What carries across the masked lane

1. **Why retiring `hair_band` without retiring `clothing` (`0604g`) broke shoulder-length hair**:
   - A `part` mask for `head, face, and hair` on a short-haired source subject stops at the original subject's nape and jawline. When the replacement still (`<Picture 1>`) has hair that reaches the shoulders, the new hair must live in the **64-px grown margin** over the subject's neck, collar, and shoulders.
