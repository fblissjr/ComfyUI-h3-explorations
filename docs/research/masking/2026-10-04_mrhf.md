### How core's tracker behaves

Read in ComfyUI core, `comfy/ldm/sam3/tracker.py` and
`comfy_extras/nodes_sam3.py` (code):

- `SAM3_VideoTrack.max_objects` defaults to 4 in the schema; 0 means
  `INTERNAL_MAX_OBJECTS`.
- Tracks are never retired. Once `total_valid_entries >= max_objects`,
  `run_det` is false and detection never runs again: no new objects, no
  reconditioning, no `keep_alive` updates. This is why tracking every person
  spends the cap on the first shots.
- Unmatched detections are admitted in score order up to the free slots.
- With text conditioning, a track that detections stop matching counts down
  (`keep_alive`), and at zero or below its mask is written as `NO_OBJ_SCORE`
  for that frame. A dip under `detection_threshold` therefore blanks the whole
  mask for a run of frames.
- A detection scoring 0.8 or more that overlaps a track by half replaces the
  tracked mask on that frame, so the outline can alternate between the
  tracker's shape and the detector's.
- With `initial_mask` and no conditioning there is no detector, no
  `keep_alive` and no replacement. The mask must be on frame 0 of the frames
  passed, so this route needs one call per shot.
- `SAM3_TrackToMask.object_indices` selects by slot. A slot is not an identity
  across a cut.

### Protecting the neighbours (design, sent to mrpink, not built)

`grow_pixels` reaches into the people standing against the lead.

- One optional mask on `MiniMaxH3MaskedSource`, `people`: every tracked person
  (`SAM3_TrackToMask` with `object_indices` empty). The node subtracts the
  lead itself.
- protect = people and not grow(lead, `feather_pixels`). The lead's own pixels
  and a feather-wide margin always win, so a replacement slightly wider than
  the original is not cut off.
- Tokens: drop a token from the regenerated set only if every pixel of it in
  every frame of its run is protect. A token holding both people stays
  regenerated.
- Composite: alpha = `pixel_alpha(tokens)` times (1 - blurred protect), which
  returns the neighbour's source pixels inside mixed tokens.
- Where the lead's long hair covered the person behind and the replacement has
  none, the model invents that strip of the neighbour and it meets real pixels
  at the protect edge.
- Checks it would need: an absent or empty `people` gives today's output
  exactly; a token with one lead pixel stays regenerated; alpha never exceeds
  the token alpha.

Whether it is needed is open. On the sheets the neighbours already look right.

## 4. The flicker in masked_v2v_generic_30s

The owner pointed at the 20 second mark, beside the black trousers. That is
the wide profile shot against the painted backdrop, frames 438 to 538; the
source is idontwannatalk.mp4.

- seen, on enlarged consecutive crops of frames 474 to 485: a grey smudge
  beside the new subject's trouser leg at knee height, inside the regenerated
  backdrop. It sits where the source singer's light striped trousers are and
  moves with his knee.
- seen: the source has no cast shadow on the backdrop there.
- measured, `cell_probe.py`, mean absolute render-minus-source per 32 px cell,
  rows y 512 to 607, frames 466 to 493: every cell from x 704 rightwards stays
  between 0.5 and 0.9, so the composite edge is fixed at x 704 and no token
  changes state. The cells x 640 to 703, regenerated backdrop, differ from the
  true backdrop by about 5 on most frames and by 1 to 2 on single frames,
  mostly the last frame of a four-frame run (470, 474, 482).
- measured, `patch_probe.py`, the patch x 640 to 672, y 500 to 600, frames 440
  to 537: mean absolute step into a frame by position in the run is 3.2 for
  the last frame and 2.19, 2.28, 2.28 for the others. Twenty-four runs; a lean,
  not proof.
- measured: away from the subject the composite equals the source (mean
  absolute difference under 1).

### Why (inferred, not proven)

- It is not the start of sampling. `h3_config.PDD8_SIGMAS` starts at 1.0 and
  the flow noise scaling is `sigma * noise + (1 - sigma) * latent`, so a
  masked token starts as pure noise (code).
- The window encodes the raw source: `z = vae.encode(src_pixels)` in
  `audio_freeze_song.py` (code). The video VAE is a 3D causal CNN encoder with
  a ViT3D decoder (`comfy/ldm/minimax/vae.py`, its header).
- So a kept token beside the subject was encoded with him inside its
  receptive field, in space and in earlier frames. It is re-injected clean at
  every step (`MiniMaxH3.scale_latent_inpaint`), and the model and the decoder
  can paint a remnant of him into the regenerated tokens next to it.
  `grow_pixels` at its default is one token of margin.
- The original moves far more than the replacement in that shot, so there is
  a lot of swept backdrop for the remnant to show on.

### The fix to test

Fill the masked pixels, per frame, over the grown mask, before `vae.encode`.
Keep the composite as it is, so true source pixels still return outside the
token mask.

- Locked camera: a per-shot temporal median of the source frames is a clean
  plate.
- Moving camera: a cheap fill from the mask's surroundings. The filled pixels
  are regenerated anyway; the fill only has to keep the original's trace out
  of the kept tokens.

The test: the same window and seed with and without the fill, then
`cell_probe.py` on both, comparing the x 640 to 703 cells. If the haze stays,
the explanation is wrong and the model is inventing it.

### A composite that shows the render only where it has to (design, not built)

mrpink's rule: keep the render where the new subject is or where the old
subject was, and restore the source everywhere else inside the regenerated
tokens. Then margin costs nothing visible and `grow_pixels` can be generous.

My suggestion for how to get that alpha without a second SAM pass: a
difference matte. Inside the regenerated tokens the render differs from the
source exactly where the new subject is or the old subject was; where the
model's invented backdrop matches the true one, the difference is small and
the source can be shown. alpha = token alpha times a soft function of the
blurred |render - source|, with holes filled. It keeps the new subject's own

### VOID for the plate with nobody in it: what its paper settles (paper)

The owner endorsed VOID for the clean plate ("good idea on void") and pointed
at the paper, kept at `internal/refs/VOID_Video_Object_and_Interaction_Deletion.md`
(gitignored). Read on 2026-10-04. It settles two things section 5 left
unverified and adds a limit.

- **A shadow outside the object mask has to be marked.** The quadmask's white
  is "areas which should stay the same" and the model is trained to "preserve
  unaffected regions"; light grey is the area affected by the removal, and
  the authors tightened it "closely on where effects take place" (section
  3.2). So the old cast shadow must lie in the light-grey region or VOID will
  keep it. Upstream finds that region with a VLM plus SAM 3 (section 3.6).
- **A coarse affected region is in distribution.** Training "gridifies" the
  affected regions, and at inference they come from a coarse grid of cells.
  So a blocky region around the subject, with no VLM, is a reasonable thing
  to try. The paper's own ablation with the whole frame light grey "degrades
  performance across all categories" (section 4, masking strategy), so the
  region should be near the subject, not everything.
- **Pass 2 is not needed here.** It is triggered only when the removal makes
  other objects move; where only photometric effects change, "the input video
  provides similarly strong constraints" (section 3.5). So `void_pass2` and
  the RAFT file can be left out.
- **Close-ups are a stated weak spot.** Limitations: domain gaps "when test
  videos have the cameras at an unusual angle or too close to the object";
  lengths "in the range of a few seconds"; resolution "could be further
  improved". The first clip's close-ups are where the cut shoulder and the
  specks are, so VOID may be weakest exactly there.
- VOID starts from Generative Omnimatte's weights, which were trained to
  remove an object with its shadows and reflections.

Still unverified: the text prompt. The upstream input format carries a
description of the scene after removal (card); the paper as fetched does not
say how much it matters. By the owner's rule it has to be generic and visible.

### Sapiens2: how heavy the lift is (code, paper)

The owner put the paper at `internal/refs/Sapiens2.md` and the repository at
`coderef/sapiens2` (checkout `7e5bae8`). Read, nothing run.

- **Dependencies are ordinary**: torch, torchvision, opencv, safetensors,
  timm, transformers, accelerate and a few utilities (`pyproject.toml`). No
  mmcv-style framework to install.
- **The model is a plain ViT plus a small head.** A self-contained backbone is
  in `sapiens/backbones/standalone/sapiens2.py`; the segmentation head and the
  matting head are a couple of hundred lines each
  (`sapiens/dense/src/models/heads/`).
- **Inference is per image**: resize and pad to 1024x768 (height by width),
  normalise, one forward pass, interpolate back. Segmentation takes the argmax
  over 29 classes; matting returns four channels, a pre-multiplied foreground
  and an alpha (`sapiens/dense/tools/vis/vis_seg.py`, `vis_matting.py`).
- **Nothing temporal.** Each frame is independent, so its output can flicker
  between frames like any per-frame model.
- The working size is portrait and the lane's frames are landscape with
  several people, so it would be run on a crop around the tracked subject, and
  a matte would be cut to that subject's SAM mask, since the matting model
  marks every person as foreground. inferred from the input size and the
  task, not tested.
- The paper's tasks are pose, body-part segmentation, pointmaps, normals and
  albedo, and it claims "pixel-accurate hair segmentation"; matting is a
  released model the paper barely mentions.

So the lift is a port of roughly a thousand lines into the pack (the rule here
is not to import Python from `coderef/`) and a check of the port against the
upstream output. Smaller than "heavy" suggested, and still only worth it if
the composite shows a hard edge or the part detection keeps missing. The
licence clause above stands.

### The latest SAM 3 commits: a caller mask at any frame, and a streaming mode (code)

The owner pointed at the newest commits in `coderef/sam3`. Four landed on
2026-09-18; two of them are refactors that prepare the third.

- **`74882c2`, "Condition the video tracker on a caller mask".** A new
  `add_mask` request seeds or refines one tracker object, named by the caller's
  own `obj_id`, from an exact binary mask at any frame
  (`add_mask` in `coderef/sam3/sam3/model/sam3_base_predictor.py`, `add_tracker_new_mask` in `coderef/sam3/sam3/model/sam3_video_inference.py`).
  A caller's mask is treated as authoritative: the object is taken out of the
  removed and suppressed sets and its masklet is confirmed outright
  (`_confirm_masklet`). That is the upstream form of what this note proposed
  for cuts: tell the tracker "this is the lead" on the first frame of each
  shot, under one id, so he stays one object and is not dropped.
- It does not reach us directly, for two reasons. It is served only by the
  instance-interactivity model; for the multiplex model, which is the 3.1
  checkpoint this lane loads, the predictor raises and says to use a point or
  box prompt instead. And ComfyUI core's port has none of it
  (`comfy/ldm/sam3/`, `comfy_extras/nodes_sam3.py` have no `add_mask`); core's
  `SAM3_VideoTrack` takes `initial_mask` on the first frame of the frames it
  is given.
- So the equivalent in core today is one tracker call per shot with the
  lead's mask as `initial_mask`, which is the per-shot seeding described in
  section 3. What still has to choose the lead's mask on each shot's first
  frame is the open part.
- **`736c957`** adds `force_tracker_propagation`, a forced pass, which the
  predictor forwards only to models that accept it, "so a multiplex model is
  unaffected".
- **`2345a4a`, "Evict streamed SAM3 frame outputs".** An opt-in
  `evict_cached_frame_outputs` drops each frame's cached output once the
  caller has it, so a long recording keeps tracker memory without
  accumulating full-resolution masks on the GPU. Useful for long clips; not a
  need at the lengths rendered here, and not in core's port either.

### Subtracting the pick frame's other people widens the gap about threefold (measured)

A second probe run (`reid_probe3.py`, saving the signatures) added the first

comparison be tried other ways offline. All on the top-third signature,

| comparison | lead facing the camera, lowest | first frame of shots 5 and 7 | lead from behind | everyone else, highest | gap |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| plain | 0.945 | 0.971, 0.977 | 0.863, 0.870 | 0.888 | +0.057 |

The other people on the pick frame are certainly not the subject, so their

### A draft of the node with less to set (not in the tree)

Status: built and checked on the CPU with stand-ins; not registered, not run

replace the two flat inputs and do not append beside them; bump
`MASK_VERSION` whenever an automatic rule changes, since the kept mask's key

The draft:

- `pick_on`: `automatic`, or `a frame I name` which reveals `pick_frame`.
  Automatic applies the pick rule on every shot's frame, groups the people
  it favours by plain top-third similarity, and takes the group covering the
  most frames. Reasoned: a lead is the largest person in the long shots, and
  a cutaway's largest person is the largest only there. The first idea, the
  frame where the rule is most decisive, would choose a cutaway's close-up.
- `match`: `automatic`, or `at a value` which reveals `match_threshold`.
  Automatic is a floor, moved up to the middle of the widest gap in the
  shots' best scores when that gap lies above the floor. The constants and
  what each was measured on are in the draft module. The report prints the
  scores in order with the cut marked.
- Each shot is first judged a few frames in.
- The preview's tiles carry the facts: every detection outlined, the one
  taken in green, an absent shot's best candidate in orange, and the shot,
  its frames, its score and picked, taken or absent written on the tile.
- The thresholds are marked advanced. The owner's frontend shows advanced
  inputs without a click (owner, 2026-10-04), so the marking hides nothing
  there; the owner said to leave it.

Untested and worth saying: the automatic pick depends on the plain
similarity telling the lead from the others across shots, which has one CPU
probe behind it; and the automatic cut has only this clip's scores behind
its floor. Both state what they chose in the report, and both have a manual
option beside them.
